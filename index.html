Sky Ace

Fly with one hand gripping a flight stick (a chopstick pressed on the table works): lean it left/right to roll, pull it back to climb, push it forward to dive. Keep pulling and you'll loop right over the top. Or press 1 to fly with a flat hand like a paper plane instead.

The other hand is throttle and weapons: raise it for speed, pinch thumb and index to fire guns, close your fist for a missile (wait for the lock tone), and turn it like a dial for rudder.

Sit about an arm's length from the webcam with both hands in view. Clap twice (or press C) any time to re-centre. You'll get a 3-second countdown to hold your flying hand level, so the game knows what "straight ahead" means. Turn your head a little to look around (L turns that off). V switches between chase and cockpit view. Keyboard always works too: arrows fly, W/S throttle, Space guns, F missile.

WW2 planes fly a less arcade model: real G limits (over-stress the wing and it breaks), energy matters more, no lead marker (walk your tracers on), limited ammo. Pinch = machine guns, fist = everything (keyboard: Space / Shift).

Duel a friend on the same Wi-Fi. Both laptops run Sky Ace (start.bat on Windows, start.command on a Mac). One hosts and leaves the address blank; the other types the address the host's screen shows. First to 5 kills; you both calibrate before every round.

Bomber escort (2+ laptops, same name/address boxes): one of you flies a Bf 109 and must bring down 3 bombers from a formation; everyone else flies P-51 escorts and must shoot the 109 down 3 times or keep it off for 5 minutes. Friendly fire is on. The 109 passes round each mission.
